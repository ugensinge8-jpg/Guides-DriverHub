Travelling in Bhutan: the essentials

What to know before you go — written in Bhutan by people who work in its tourism. Rules and fees change, so each section points to the official source.

Meet Bhutan's guides

Visas and the Sustainable Development Fee

Most visitors need a visa arranged before they travel; Indian, Bangladeshi and Maldivian nationals follow a separate permit process. Visas are applied for online through Bhutan's official system, usually by your tour operator.

Visitors also pay a Sustainable Development Fee (SDF) for each night in the country, which funds conservation, health and education. It is currently US$100 per adult per night for most nationalities (it was US$200 until 2023); children and Indian nationals pay different rates. Check the current figures with the Department of Tourism before you book.

Do you need a guide?

For most visitors, yes: a licensed Bhutanese guide is required for travel beyond Thimphu and Paro and for all treks. Guides are licensed by the Department of Tourism. A good guide is the heart of a Bhutan trip — they explain the dzongs, festivals and customs, and handle permits along the way.

Getting there

Paro International Airport is the country's only international airport, served by Drukair and Bhutan Airlines from cities in the region. Overland, visitors cross at Phuentsholing, Gelephu or Samdrup Jongkhar from India.

When to go

Spring (March to May)

Rhododendrons in bloom, mild days, and spring tshechus such as Paro's.

Autumn (September to November)

Clear mountain views and the busiest festival season, including Thimphu's tshechu.

Winter (December to February)

Cold, crisp and clear in the west; black-necked cranes winter in Phobjikha.

Monsoon (June to August)

Green and quiet, with rain and occasional road delays — fewer visitors.

Where to go

Paro

The airport valley, Rinpung Dzong, and the hike to Taktsang, the Tiger's Nest monastery.

Thimphu

The capital: Tashichho Dzong, the Buddha Dordenma, markets and crafts.

Punakha

Punakha Dzong at the meeting of two rivers, reached over the Dochula pass.

Phobjikha (Gangtey)

A glacial valley, Gangtey Monastery and the winter cranes.

Bumthang

Central Bhutan's temples and valleys, often called its spiritual heartland.

Haa

A quiet western valley over the Chele La pass.

Good to know

  • The currency is the ngultrum, pegged to the Indian rupee.
  • Dress modestly in dzongs and temples — long sleeves and covered legs — and remove hats.
  • Walk clockwise around chortens, prayer wheels and temples.
  • Ask before photographing people, and inside temples photography is usually not allowed.
  • Mountain roads are slow and winding: a short distance can take hours, which your itinerary should allow for.

Questions

How much is the SDF in Bhutan?

The Sustainable Development Fee is currently US$100 per adult per night for most nationalities, with different rates for children and Indian nationals. Check bhutan.travel for the current figures.

Do I need a guide in Bhutan?

For most visitors, a licensed Bhutanese guide is required for travel beyond Thimphu and Paro and for all treks.

What is the best time to visit Bhutan?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the most popular, with clear views and festivals. Winter is cold but clear; the monsoon months are green and quiet.

How do I get to Bhutan?

Fly into Paro International Airport with Drukair or Bhutan Airlines, or cross overland from India at Phuentsholing, Gelephu or Samdrup Jongkhar.
